The headline finding here is a text/image mismatch in the input itself, and it needs to be accounted for before judging whether Gemma4's answer is "right" or "wrong."

The case text matches, almost word for word, a real published case report: Ozlu et al., "Case Report: Lupoid cutaneous leishmaniasis mimicking verruca plana," F1000Research, 2017. Figure 1 in that paper shows a cluster of small yellow-brown bumps, each only a few millimeters wide, grouped together on the cheek — not a single large mass, and nothing on the nose. The uploaded image here shows something quite different: one large, crusted, verrucous-looking mass covering essentially the whole nose, with no visible lesion on the cheek at all. That doesn't match Figure 1, Figure 3 (the same paper's post-treatment cheek photo), or Figure 2 (a Giemsa-stained microscopy slide) — the only three figures the paper actually contains. In short, the photo fed to the model is very likely from a different leishmaniasis case entirely, not from the same source as the text.

This matters because the model's own self-report is consistent with that mismatch. Unlike the previous Syrian-MCL case, where "Evidence source used by the model" was "Both" and the answer described specific visual findings, this run reports "Evidence source used by the model: Medical knowledge" only, and the single image-related line in the output ("the image quality is insufficient for definitive histological or microscopic diagnosis") is generic — no mention of the nose, no mention of a verrucous or crusted surface, nothing indicating the model actually engaged with what's in the photo. That reads as the model not meaningfully using the image this time, possibly because the text said "cheek" while the image showed something anatomically different.

There's a second, separate issue: the text given to the model also omits the single most diagnostic sentence from the original case. Two years before this cheek presentation, the same patient had been treated for cutaneous leishmaniasis on the nose with an antimony injection, and that lesion had cleared completely — the new cheek papules appearing months later are the classic recurrence pattern for lupoid CL. The text passed into this run stops at "presented with multiple papular lesions on the left cheek... gradually enlarged" and excludes that history. Without it, "multiple slowly enlarging papules on a child's cheek" is genuinely nonspecific — it overlaps with warts, molluscum, granuloma annulare, lupus vulgaris, and more — so a clinician given only that one sentence would also be reluctant to commit.

Given both factors, I'd call the model's behavior reasonably well-calibrated rather than simply wrong: Low confidence, "Dermatitis/Inflammatory Dermatosis, pending further information" as the top line, Mucocutaneous Leishmaniasis kept at Rank 2, and biopsy/tissue sampling explicitly requested. The actual diagnosis, established once a Giemsa-stained skin smear turned up Leishmania amastigotes, was lupoid cutaneous leishmaniasis — so the model didn't get the correct top-1 label, but it wasn't confidently wrong either; it flagged real uncertainty and kept leishmaniasis on the list, which is a defensible posture given how little usable signal it was actually handed.
