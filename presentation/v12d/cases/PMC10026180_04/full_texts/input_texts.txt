An 11-year-old Arab girl with a known diagnosis of ataxia telangiectasia (diagnosed at 1 year) 
presented with a spreading skin rash on her nose that extended to the nostrils and her left cheek. 
Family history included consanguineous parents and another sibling with AT. Her childhood course 
included ataxia, recurrent lung and ear infections, sclerosing cholangitis, hypothyroidism, and later 
diffuse large B‑cell lymphoma. She used a wheelchair and had multiple café‑au‑lait spots and 
telangiectasias. Immunologic testing showed a decreased immunoglobulin level (IgG 516 mg/L) with 
lymphocyte count and function reported as normal. Her baseline therapies included monthly IVIG, 
inhaled budesonide and fluticasone, and levothyroxine.
Initially she received amoxicillin–clavulanic acid and topical chloramphenicol without clinical response. 
On examination, the tip of the nose had an erythematous plaque with a central erosion and a 
circinate‑elevated border with overlying scattered pustules; the nose bridge and nostrils had small 
erythematous erosions. An atypical infection was considered.
