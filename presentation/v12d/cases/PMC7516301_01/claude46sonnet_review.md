On the positives: the model grounded in both modalities — it explicitly cited the image as confirming papillomatous mucosal lesions rather than just leaning on retrieved text — so the multimodal fusion part of your pipeline is doing real work here. The top call, mucocutaneous leishmaniasis with prior cutaneous exposure, is also directionally right; the lesion genuinely is leishmanial.

Where it falls short is the actual point of the source case. The causative organism, Leishmania tropica, is an Old World species that normally causes self-healing skin disease, not mucosal involvement. Labeling this generically as "MCL," a term more conventionally tied to New World L. braziliensis disease, papers over the species/pattern mismatch that made the case worth publishing in the first place. More importantly, the model's differential and "Diagnosis Assessment" never reach the actual clinical pearl: this patient's leishmaniasis was severe and recurrent because of an underlying immune defect. He relapsed three times over roughly two years despite treatment with liposomal amphotericin B and miltefosine, which is what prompted an immunodeficiency workup — initially read as CVID, until whole-genome sequencing traced it to a disease-causing CD40 ligand gene mutation, identifying the rare primary immunodeficiency Hyper IgM syndrome. So the clinically correct "needed next input" isn't just histopathology confirmation of leishmaniasis, which the model did say — it's "investigate for primary immunodeficiency," and that's entirely missing.

That gap traces to corpus design, not model reasoning. Your demo KB is leishmaniasis-only — 4 of 6 retrieved candidates, all leishmaniasis-positive — so there's no contrastive evidence that could ever point toward "the recurrence/severity pattern itself is the abnormal signal." The rank-2/rank-3 alternatives (fungal/viral infection, dermatitis) are coming from Gemma4's parametric knowledge, not retrieval; none of the four retrieved chunks mention immune status or relapse pattern. That's worth a line in your limitations section: a single-disease retrieval corpus can produce a confident, well-evidenced-looking answer that still misses the diagnosis that actually mattered clinically.
