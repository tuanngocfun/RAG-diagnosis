A 37‑year‑old woman from eastern Bhutan presented in 2014 with 
extensive erythematous plaques on the forehead, central face, 
perioral area, and cheeks extending to both ears. The left eye was 
damaged and there was marked swelling of the right eyelid with lid 
scarring and lagophthalmos. Plaques extended to the nasal 
mucosa without intraoral involvement. Other skin and systemic 
examinations were reportedly normal.
Laboratory testing showed hemoglobin 11.7 g/dl, total leukocyte 
count 6,390/mm3 (neutrophils 62%, lymphocytes 28%), platelets 
191,000/mm3, ESR 39 mm in the 1st hour, SGOT 35 U/L, SGPT 68 
U/L, bilirubin 1 mg/dl, urea 21 mg/dl, creatinine 0.8 mg/dl, fasting 
glucose 80 mg/dl. Tests for HIV, hepatitis B and C, and syphilis were 
negative. Chest radiograph, ECG, and abdominal ultrasound were 
normal.
Relevant past history: in February 1999, during pregnancy she had 
fever, weight loss, anemia, and hepatomegaly and “was diagnosed 
as VL and treated with SSG injections 850 mg/day for 20 days.” 
Seven months after that illness she developed erythematous 
lesions on her forehead that spread over the face. In 2002 a 
dermatologist recorded papules and plaques over the cheeks, 
periorbital, and perioral areas that “were suspected as chronic 
dermatitis or cutaneous lupus erythematosus,” and she received 
topical, intralesional, and oral corticosteroids. In 2007 a WHO 
consultant for kala azar “suspected PKDL.” Skin biopsies performed 
in 2006, 2010, and 2013 “failed to confirm leishmaniasis,” and no 
firm diagnosis had been recorded.
